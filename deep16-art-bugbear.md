# The Bugbear: four 16-bit sprite sheets

Four sprite sheets of one character, a foe in a 16-bit tactics game. Draw each sheet as its own image, in order. Sheet 1 sets the look. Sheets 2, 3 and 4 draw the very same bugbear as sheet 1: the same character, the same pixel style, the same colours, the same size as sheet 1's turnaround. If sheet 1 is attached, match it exactly.

## The bugbear (on every sheet)

A big, hairy goblinoid, taller than a man and stooped, with long powerful arms that hang to its knees. Coarse shaggy brown fur over yellow-brown hide, a shaggy ruff of darker fur round its jaw, a broad flat bear-like nose, a wide mouth with jutting yellow fangs, small fierce yellow eyes under a heavy brow, and pointed ears. Rough hide armour of stitched furs, a dark leather belt, bare clawed feet. A spiked iron morningstar in its right hand, a round wooden shield bound in hide strapped to its left forearm, and a bundle of three javelins slung across its back. A brute, but a sneaky one: it likes to wait in hiding and strike first.

## The style (on every sheet)

16-bit pixel art animation sheet, SNES-era action RPG style, crisp dark outline, readable at small size. Flat dark navy background (a deep blue, not grey and not black). Labelled rows of frames, each row's name beside it, numbered 1 to N under each frame -- every number used once, none skipped, one drawing for each number. Every frame stands apart with clear space around it: nothing overlaps a neighbour or a label. The same scale in every row, feet on one baseline. No javelin drawn in flight anywhere. No scenery except what a row names.

## Sheet 1: the look, side view

Top left: one large portrait. Beside it a turnaround of four stills labelled Front, Right, Back, Left. Below: these rows, every frame in side view facing right.

- **Idle** (6 frames): standing stooped, its long arms hanging, breathing slowly, its ears twitching and its eyes narrowed, the morningstar swaying low at its side.
- **Walk** (8 frames): a heavy, loping, stooped stride, the long arms swinging, the shield held at its side.
- **Morningstar** (6 frames): it raises the morningstar high and brings it down hard on a foe in front of it, then back on guard.
- **Javelin** (6 frames): the morningstar hung at its belt, it pulls a javelin from the bundle on its back, draws back and hurls it overhand at a foe in front of it; the last frame shows the empty throwing hand following through.
- **Flinch** (4 frames): struck, it jolts back with a snarl, its eyes squeezed shut and its ears flattened, then recovers.
- **Fall** (6 frames): knocked out, it topples backward and lies flat on its back, the morningstar dropped beside it; the last frame lies on the ground.
- **Prone** (2 frames): knocked flat but alive and struggling: lying on the ground, then pushing itself up.

## Sheet 2: its tricks, side view

No portrait and no turnaround: the rows only, every frame in side view facing right, the same size as sheet 1's turnaround.

- **Lurk** (4 frames): crouched low and perfectly still in hiding, the shield pulled in close, only its slow breathing and its watching eyes moving.
- **Ambush** (6 frames): it bursts up out of a low crouch with both hands on the morningstar's haft and brings it down on a foe in front of it in one huge surprise blow, then straightens with a fanged grin.
- **Climb** (6 frames): climbing straight up an unseen wall with its long arms and its feet, the morningstar hung at its belt; do not draw the wall.
