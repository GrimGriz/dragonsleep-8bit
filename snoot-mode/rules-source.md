# The Snoot mode's rules source -- CHAINMAIL and OD&D, as read

*The version of the mechanics the Snoot mode is built on, stored where a seat can read it in-repo. Written 2026-10-13/14 by the Cowork seat (config echo claude-fable-5-1) from the four PDFs Griz handed over -- "pretty sure he had cobbled it together from these pdfs" -- the ones his real-life table's Chef worked from: CHAINMAIL, Rules for Medieval Miniatures, 3rd edition, 7th printing (Gygax & Perren, TSR, 1979) and DUNGEONS & DRAGONS Books I-III (Gygax & Arneson, TSR, 1974). The books themselves are in `dev/chainmail-odd/` (local, gitignored, beside the SRD PDF; the OD&D text layers as `.txt` there too). **This is a reading, not a transcription:** the SRD 5.1 could go into `srd/` as markdown because it is CC-BY; these are TSR/WotC copyright and this repo deploys publicly, so the rules are restated here in our words with the books' own printed page numbers, and the books stay off the repo. Where a number is a game fact (a movement rate, a morale rating) it is given; where the book's expression is the thing (the combat grids, the spell list's wording) the shape is given and the page cited. Rulings on what the mode takes are Griz's, 10-13, and are marked RULED; the seat's readings are marked so.*

**Rulings that frame this (Griz, 10-13):** the player runs every non-gnoll force, the gnoll side is the AI, the non-gnoll AI later (*"build for all, a.i. later"*) · a hex fight with no party in it resolves by the Combat Tables on the hex (*"Chef's dice"*), DEEP16 only where the party stands · written orders for every unit, then the turn resolves, for the first build (*"might let the party move in-between resolves but we'll build it straight that way first"*) · the world map is revised and clarified before any hex is drawn · the mode's own folder and door (a lean, his and the seat's) · his three rules: the witness fill, the collision, the combine-units move (§9).

## 1. The two clocks

**The map turn is a day** (OD&D Book III, *The Wilderness*, pp. 16-17). The wilderness is a hex map; the greatest distance across a hex is about five miles; each move constitutes one day, and each day is a turn. Movement in hexes a day: a man on foot 3; a wagon or cart 4; a draft horse 5; a heavy horse 6; a medium horse 8; a light horse 10; a raft 10 (3 in swamp); a boat 15 (5); a merchant ship 12; a galley 20 (6); flyers from a dragon's 24 to a roc's or pegasus's 48. Terrain (p. 16, after OUTDOOR SURVIVAL): mountains and swamps cost 3 a hex, a river crossed off a ford 3, woods and desert 2; a track through mountains 2, a track through woods or swamp nothing. Parties over 100 lose a hex a day, over 1,000 two. Every creature rests a full day after six days of movement. The register's road scale (17 miles a cart-day; the inn midway on 35 miles) is this scale: the inn to Silverton is a foot-day, three hexes.

**The battle on a hex is minutes** (CHAINMAIL, p. 8): one figure stands for twenty men, one inch on the table is ten yards, and a turn of play is about one minute of battle.

**Which rules a fight uses** (Book III, *Land Combat*, p. 25): the basic system is CHAINMAIL's at one figure to one man or creature; big battles are fought at 20:1, with "single fantastic types" fighting separately at 1:1 or against a single 20:1 figure. **The mode's reading:** the 20:1 battle is the Combat Tables on the hex (§5); the 1:1 is DEEP16, entered when the party's unit is in the hex. RULED: Chef's dice for the party-less fight.

## 2. The turn (CHAINMAIL, p. 9)

Two systems. **Move / counter-move:** both roll a die, the higher chooses to move first or last; the first side moves, split-moves and fires with any pass-through fire against it; the second side the same; artillery; missiles; melees; repeat. Missile fire from split-moving troops and pass-through fire take effect during movement; all other fire just before melee. **Simultaneous movement:** both sides write orders for every unit (direction and facing); both move half, checking for unordered contacts and conducting split-moves, missile fire and pass-through; the balance of the move; artillery; missiles; melees. A unit may be ordered "charge if charged" and moves half, then up to 12" in support. Morale checks fall wherever the rules require them in either system. **RULED for the first build: written orders then resolve** -- the simultaneous system; the party moving between resolves is a later lever.

## 3. Terrain, movement, formation, fatigue (CHAINMAIL, pp. 9-11)

**Terrain:** a hill halves movement and forbids charges (downhill at normal speed); woods as a hill and no formed bodies move through; marsh as a hill and no heavy equipment, catapults or guns enter; rough ground forbids charges; ditch and rampart as a hill; a stream costs 6" to cross and forbids a charge; a river halts a unit before and after and costs a whole move. Terrain can be drawn from a deck of index cards (p. 10: two rivers, a marsh, a pond, a gully, two woods, a rough, four hills, eight blanks; the weaker side draws first).

**Movement in inches** (p. 10; charge move in brackets; road bonus +3 for catapults and wagons): armored foot 6 (6); heavy foot 9 (12), missile range 3" for thrown axes and spears; landsknechte and Swiss 12 (15); light foot and archers 9 (12), range 15; crossbowmen 12 (12), range 18; longbowmen 12 (15), range 21; Turk archers 9 (12), 21; heavy crossbowmen 9 (12), 24; heavy horse 12 (18); medium horse 18 (24), range 15; light horse 24 (30), range 18; catapults and wagons 6.

**Formation and facing** (pp. 10-11): column to line or line to column costs a move, column to square a move, line to square two (foot only); a unit caught in melee mid-change is flanked. Facing: oblique a quarter move, a left or right face half, an about-face a whole move. Poorly trained troops pay double, Swiss and horse half.

**Fatigue** (p. 11): five consecutive turns moving; or two turns moving then a charge then a melee; or one move, a charge and two melee rounds; or three rounds of melee -- the unit is fatigued: it attacks and defends at the next lower class and its morale value drops one. Uphill movement counts double. One turn of rest restores it.

## 4. Missile fire (CHAINMAIL, pp. 11-13)

A table of casualties by the number of figures firing against the target's armor (unarmored, half-armor or shield, fully armored) with a die to decide; larger groups split into equal groups no bigger than the table's maximum (p. 11). Rates: crossbows, archers and longbows fire every turn, twice if they neither move nor are meleed; moved up to half, once; more than half, only by beating the enemy's die; heavy crossbows every other turn. Horse archers split-move and fire (p. 12). Pass-through fire: stationary missile troops shoot at anything passing within range during the enemy's half-move. Arcs 45 degrees each side for foot, 180 and 45 for horse. Two ranks fire; indirect fire by archers and longbowmen over intervening troops at two-thirds range, the target counted one armor class heavier. Cover halves missile casualties; woods and roofs stop indirect fire. Thrown weapons once a turn, always at chargers, never indirect. Catapults (p. 12): light 15"-30" range and a 2" hit area every other turn, heavy 24"-48" and 3½" every third, a crew of four; a called range and a triangulation; a two-dice over/under option. Gunpowder (pp. 13-14): arquebusiers kill anything they hit on an accuracy die by range; cannon by a dowel of alternating sections -- noted, not the mode's.

## 5. Melee (CHAINMAIL, pp. 14-17; Appendix A, p. 40; Appendix E, p. 44)

**Classes:** light foot (missile troops, Swiss and landsknechte, peasants, crews); heavy foot (Normans, Saxons, Turks, Vikings, men-at-arms); armored foot (dismounted knights, sergeants, Italian levies, condottiere); light horse (Magyars, Mongols, Saracens, Spanish, Turks, hobilars); medium horse (Mongols, Norman knights, esquires, Saracens, Turcopoles); heavy horse (knights, Reiter, gendarmes) (pp. 14-15).

**The Combat Tables** (Appendix A, p. 40): for each attacking class against each defending class, a number of dice per figure (or one die per two, three or four figures) and the scores that kill. The shape, as anchors: a class against its own kind rolls one die a man and kills on a 6; a heavier class against a lighter one rolls more dice a man (heavy horse against light foot: four dice a man, kills on 5-6) or kills on wider scores; a lighter class against a heavier one rolls fewer (light foot against heavy horse: one die per four men, kills on 6). Pikes and halberds add a die; pikemen in close order (five by two at least) fight as armored foot with a mass-shock die. The full grid is on p. 40 of the local copy; the mode's table is a transcription of its numbers into a data file, which is where the expression stops being the book's.

**Charges** (p. 15): a charge is permitted only when contact is expected that turn; it must continue the following turn at normal speed; cavalry charges in a nearly straight line; the winner must carry the balance of the charge forward. **Impetus** (p. 17): heavy foot, armored foot and all horse add a die when they charge across smooth level ground or a gentle slope. **Flank** (p. 16): the attacker counts the next class up (heavy horse +1 a die); **rear**: no return casualties and the flank bonus. **Missile troops in a line** refuse combat and fall back 3" (p. 15). Casualties come off before morale. Missiles never go into a melee; every unit controls 1" either side of itself; **a unit within 3" of a melee may be drawn in at its owner's choice, moving up to 6"** (p. 16); after the first round excess figures swing to the enemy's flanks and rear. Standing horse defends a class down (p. 17). A hedgehog of ten or more pikemen moves at half, never charges, cannot be drawn in (p. 17). Prisoners (pp. 16-17): the loser gives up prisoners by a die, one on a retreat, two on a rout, by a table of winner and loser types; Swiss, landsknechte and Turks take none; a guard of one per five.

**The Fantasy Combat Table** (Appendix E, p. 44): fantastic figure against fantastic figure -- dragon, elemental, treant, giant, hero, lycanthrope, roc, super-hero, troll or ogre, wight or ghoul, wizard, wraith -- on two dice against a number by the pairing: under, no effect; equal, the defender falls back a move; over, killed. A hero, super-hero or wraith may withdraw on a no-effect. Against men and lesser creatures each type has its own rule (§7).

**Man-to-man** (pp. 25-26; Appendix B, p. 41): one figure one man; weapons in twelve classes from dagger to pike; the first blow goes to the attacker unless the defender's weapon is two classes higher or he fights from above; a lighter weapon strikes more blows a round; parries by class difference; mounted men +1 and footmen -1 against them; a two-dice kill table by weapon against armor type. **This is DEEP16's job in the mode** and is not rebuilt.

## 6. Morale -- the Snoot's engine (CHAINMAIL, pp. 15-21)

**Post-melee morale** (pp. 15-16): the side with fewer casualties scores the difference multiplied by a die; the side with more survivors scores the difference in survivors; each side adds its survivors multiplied by a morale rating -- peasants 3, light foot and levies 4, heavy foot 5, elite heavy foot 6, light horse 6, armored foot and janissaries 7, medium horse and landsknechte 8, heavy horse and Swiss pikemen 9 (Chinese, Korean and Japanese ratings p. 21); the side with the lower total reacts by the gap: 0-19 the melee continues; 20-39 back half a move in good order; 40-59 back a move in good order; 60-79 retreat a move; 80-99 rout a move and a half; 100 and over surrender. Melees under twenty figures a side double everything. The winner may continue a charge, leaving guards for prisoners.

**Retreat and rout** (p. 16): the unit stands unmoved next turn while it rallies; attacked while rallying it needs a 1-2 to rally and suffers casualties without return; **a retreating unit that contacts friends stops them, and both spend a turn rallying; a routing unit that contacts friends routs them too**; a unit that keeps retreating or routing rallies by turn: the first automatically if not attacked, the second on 3-6, the third on a 6, the fourth it is removed; off the table, removed.

**Instability** (p. 17): a unit that has lost a set share of its original strength rolls two dice at the end of the fire or melee segment that took it over the line -- light foot, peasants and levies at 25% need 8 or better; heavy foot a third, 7; elite heavy foot, armored foot and Mongols a third, 6; medium horse a third, 7; Swiss pikemen half, 5; heavy horse and Norman knights half, 6; mounted knights half, 4 -- and is removed on a failure; a surrounded unit that fails surrenders.

**Charges and morale** (p. 18): a pike charge forces the charged unit to roll on the loss table as if over its line; failure is a retreat of a move and a half, not removal. A cavalry charge makes the defender roll two dice against a number by its type and the horse's weight -- peasants 9, 10, 11 against light, medium, heavy; light foot and levies 8, 9, 10; heavy foot 7, 8, 9; elite heavy and armored foot 6, 7, 8; light horse 5, 6, 7; medium horse 4, 5, 6; heavy horse 3, 4, 5 -- flank -1, rear -2; both sides charging add 1 for foot, 2 for horse; pikes and pole-arms facing the charge stand automatically.

**The army commander** (p. 21): with a body of troops he adds 1 to every score that unit must make and shares its fate; any unit within 12" adds 1 to its rolls; he automatically rallies a unit he joins, if he stays three turns; **if he is killed or captured every unit on his side checks morale at once, at -2**; he is always the last figure killed in his unit; mercenaries ignore him.

**Historical characteristics** (pp. 18-20, optional): knights charge any enemy in reach regardless of orders unless a 6 is rolled on an obedience die, with a precedence list (other knights first); levies as heavy foot, Flemish +1 on morale; **mercenaries roll a die each turn: 1 they stand and demand pay, 2 they march off, 3 they are bribed and join the enemy, 4-6 they obey for three moves**; peasants need 7 on two dice to move at all and a score by enemy type to attack or defend, and fight as light foot; religious orders never surrender and take +1 on morale; berserkers +2 on attack dice, charge on sight and fight until all are dead; English longbowmen plant stakes after two turns in place, 4-6 impales a charging horse; national notes (Magyars, Mongols, Poles, Russians, Saracens, Scots schiltrons, Spanish). **Baggage** (p. 21) and **weather** (p. 22, a die each even turn across clear, cloudy, rainy) are options.

## 7. The Fantasy Supplement (CHAINMAIL, pp. 28-39; Appendix D, p. 43)

**Scale and line-up** (p. 28, p. 39): figure sizes by kind; a general line-up of Law (halflings, dwarves, gnomes, heroes, super-heroes, wizards, treants, magic weapons), Neutral (sprites, pixies, elves, fairies, lycanthropes, giants, rocs, elementals, chimerae -- neutrals diced for, ties stay out) and Chaos (goblins, kobolds, orcs, anti-heroes, wizards, wraiths, wights, lycanthropes, ogres, true trolls, giants, dragons, basilisks).

**Creatures** (pp. 29-37; the reference table p. 43 gives move, charge, fly, missile range, attack and defend for each):
- **Halflings**: scouts, blend in; three count as missile fire for every two; morale 5. **Sprites**: invisible, no casualties the first melee round; fly three turns; morale 3.
- **Dwarves and gnomes**: equal by day and night; trolls, ogres and giants score only half kills on them; attack goblins and kobolds on sight; no obedience die; morale 5, 2 points.
- **Goblins and kobolds**: in full daylight -1 on morale and every die; attack dwarves on sight; **hobgoblins fight as armored foot and defend as heavy foot, 2½ points**; morale 5.
- **Elves and fairies**: split-move and fire though on foot; invisible unless located; with magic weapons an extra die in normal combat and bonuses against fantastic kinds (three dice against goblins, two against orcs; set scores on the fantasy table against heroes, super-heroes, wizards, wraiths, wights, lycanthropes, ogres, giants); morale 6, 4 points.
- **Orcs**: light-shy as goblins; factious -- units of different kinds within a charge move of each other attack each other unless a 4 or better on an obedience die; giant orcs as armored foot; morale 5, 2 points.
- **Heroes and anti-heroes** (p. 30): the fighting ability of four figures of whatever class their arms make them; never check morale; add 1 to the die or dice of the unit they are with; the last figure in the unit to die; killed by regular troops only on four simultaneous hits, by fantastic kinds on the fantasy table; **may act independently of their command to fight another individual character**; a hero with a bow kills a passing dragon on 10 or better with two dice (rangers +1); morale 20, 20 points.
- **Super-heroes**: as heroes doubled (eight figures); **every enemy unit within their charge move must check morale as if it had taken excess casualties**; a bow kills a dragon on 8 or better; morale 40, 50 points.
- **Wizards** (pp. 30-33; sorcerers -1, warlocks -2, magicians -3, seers -4): fight as two armored foot or two medium horse; invisible at will; see in darkness; affect friendly and enemy morale as super-heroes; impervious to normal missiles, killed by another wizard's missile unless 7 or better on two dice; two missiles, a fire ball (a large catapult's hit area) and a lightning bolt (¾" by 6", a heavy field gun), range 24", with the exceptions saved -- heroes 9+, super-heroes 6+ (corrected 10-15 from the page; first read 7+), wraiths 7+, giants driven back or routed, dragons back a move, elementals by kind; sixteen spells with complexity values (phantasmal forces, darkness, wizard light, detection, concealment, conjuring an elemental, moving terrain, protection from evil, levitate, slowness, haste, polymorph, confusion, hallucinatory terrain, cloudkill, anti-magic shell); a stationary caster; spells known by rank from a wizard's six or seven to a seer's one; counter-spells on 7 or better for the stronger, 8-11 for the weaker; a complexity table of immediate, delayed and negated results by rank; morale 50 down to 20; 100 to 50 points.
- **Wraiths** (pp. 33-34): see in darkness; **raise the morale of friendly troops as heroes do; cause the enemy to check morale as super-heroes do; paralyze any enemy man they touch** until a friendly elf, hero or wizard touches him; fly; melee as two armored foot or two medium horse; harmed only by magic weapons or fantastic combat; morale 10, 10 points.
- **Lycanthropes** (p. 34): werebears and werewolves with a pack of their animal; doubled inside or within 6" of a wood; attack as four armored foot, defend as four heavy foot; four simultaneous hits to kill, a magic weapon one; see in darkness; morale 20, 20 points.
- **Trolls and ogres** (p. 34): ogres fight in formation as six heavy foot and see in darkness; killed by six accumulated hits, three from elves or heroes, one from a magic weapon; true trolls only on the fantasy table (heroes 10+, super-heroes 8+, elementals 6+, giants 10+, magic weapons 8+); true trolls fight alone, never check morale, fight as giants on the fantasy table; ogres morale 8 and 15 points, trolls 75 points.
- **Giants** (pp. 34-35): melee as twelve heavy foot with an extra die; defend as twelve armored foot and need that many hits; throw rocks as light catapults at 20" with no minimum range, and move on turns they do not throw; never check morale; 50 points.
- **Treants** (p. 35): call up to two trees within 6"; as six armored foot; immune to normal missile and melee; set afire by men on 10+, heroes 9+, super-heroes 7+; morale 20, 15 points.
- **Dragons** (p. 35): see in darkness; **every enemy unit within 15" checks morale as if over its line**; detect the hidden within 15"; fly 25"; as four heavy horse, immune to normal hits; fire in a cone ½" at the mouth to 3" at 9", killing all but dragons, super-heroes and wizards (saved on 7+); three breaths then a turn grounded; attack by preference other dragons, giants, rocs, trolls, elementals, treants; never check morale; 100 points; other colours breathe lightning (blue) or cold (white), acid (black), gas (green), a purple tail-stinger.
- **Rocs, wyverns, griffons** (pp. 35-36): attack as four light horse, defend as four heavy horse; enemy checks as against heroes; detect hidden at 48"; carry a man 36"; 20 points. **Elementals** (p. 36): one of each kind at a time, conjured; air flies 24" and attacks as four light horse (+2 against the airborne); earth moves 6", four heavy horse (+1 against the earthbound); fire 12", four medium horse (+2 against fire-users); water 6" or 18" in water, four light horse ashore and four heavy horse afloat (+2 near water); immune to normal attacks; an uncontrolled one attacks its summoner in a straight line. **Basilisk**: petrifying gaze unless 6+ (wizards, super-heroes); defends as a lycanthrope. **Giant spiders and insects**: decided ahead of play (a spider that kills on a failed save of 8+ and fights fantastic kinds as a lycanthrope, for instance). **Giant wolves**: attack as light horse, move as medium horse; carry goblins at heavy-horse speed; against fantastic kinds they count as men and need a wight's hits. **Wights and ghouls** (p. 37): attack as light horse, defend as heavy horse; immune to normal missiles; see in darkness, -1 by day; paralyze by touch for a turn; zombies attack as orcs and move as goblins; morale 10, 10 points.

**Magical weapons** (p. 38): enchanted arrows roll two dice on the fantasy table against fantastic targets, 10 points each; magic swords +1 on the fantasy table, an extra die in normal combat, a 12" light; super-swords +2 or +3; magic armor -1 on an opponent's fantasy dice, -3 man-to-man. **Air movement** by dowel and base. **Catapults** harm only the listed kinds (halflings through rocs). **Combination figures** (an Elric) with care. **Fantastic sieges** (pp. 38-39): dwarves dig twice as fast, orcs one and a half; damage against works -- trolls 2, ogres 1, giants 3, treants 2, wizards 3 (and fire), earth elementals 6.

**Point values** (p. 27): peasant ½, levy ¾, light foot 1, heavy foot 2, armored foot 2½, light horse 3, medium horse 4, heavy horse 5; pike +1, crossbow or arquebus +1½, bow +3, longbow +4, light catapult 15, heavy 20, bombard 30; mercenaries at half. **Jousting** (pp. 26-27, Appendix C, p. 42): an aiming point against a defensive position on a matrix; tourney scoring -- not the mode's. **Sieges** (pp. 22-24): defense points by work (towers 40-60 ... mantlets 3), damage by weapon (bombard 6 ... small catapult 2), towers and rams at 1" a man pushing, ladders, boiling oil, rocks, mines and counter-mines, breaches crossed at rough-ground speed -- the Pit's walls, if it comes to that.

## 8. The OD&D books -- what the mode takes

**Book I, Men & Magic:** fighting capability is a key to the CHAINMAIL fantasy rules (p. 18): a fighting-man's level names his table class -- Hero at 4th, Super-hero at 8th -- and the "alternative" d20 system against armor class is the one DEEP16 already runs. Elves and dwarves keep their CHAINMAIL advantages against certain fantastic kinds; invisibility, fire ball and the elemental's control are explained by reference to CHAINMAIL. **So the party at 8th and 9th are Super-heroes on the hex, and GreyFang (ranger 11) is one too.**

**Book II, Monsters & Treasure:** **gnolls** are "similar to hobgoblins" -- armored foot to attack, heavy foot to defend -- **with +2 morale**; **a gnoll king and his bodyguard of one to four fight as trolls without the regeneration**. Orcs come in tribes with inter-tribal hostility, lair in caves with sentries or villages with a ditch, a palisade, a light catapult per fifty and a tower, and may have leader types (a 7th-9th level fighter, an 11th-level magic-user, a dragon, ogres, trolls) by lair; they defend a lair without morale checks until outnumbered. Ogres hit for a die +2; trolls regenerate three a turn from the third round and must be burned.

**Book III, The Underworld & Wilderness Adventures:** the referee's hex map and the players' blank one, filled in hex by hex as they move (p. 16); the movement table and terrain (§1); sighting at 40-240 yards, surprise at 10-30 with three or more monsters circling the party (p. 17); lost parties move by a die; **a wandering-monster check at the end of every day by terrain** -- clear on a 6, woods and river 5-6, swamp and mountains 4-6, desert 5-6, city 6 -- and a type table by terrain (men, flyers, giant-kinds, lycanthropes, animals, swimmers, undead, dragons) with sub-tables (the giant-kinds run kobolds, goblins, orcs, hobgoblins, **gnolls**, ogres, trolls, giants, gnomes, dwarves, elves, treants; the men bandits, brigands, berserkers, lords, wizards, priests; pp. 17-18); a fighting-man, magic-user or cleric met in the wild brings 2-12 men of 1st to 4th level; castles and their tolls (p. 15: a fighter demands a joust by the CHAINMAIL rules or 100-600 gold; a magic-user sends passersby on a geas; a cleric tithes); castle guards of 30-180, half crossbow light foot and half heavy foot; **evasion** (p. 19) by party size against the share of the monsters' possible number, 5% to 90%, doubled by surprise, void if surprised unless magic or woods, +25% in woods, ±25% for double speed; **pursuit** in a random direction, a faster pursuer catching on a 50%, a half-day's rest owed for each hex pursued; land combat at 20:1 and 1:1 (§1); **time**: a wilderness move is a day, a dungeon expedition a week (p. 35).

## 10. What the mode does not take
